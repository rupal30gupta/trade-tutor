Trading Risk Management

Understand potential losses and learn why managing risk matters in financial markets.

1. What Is Risk Management?

Risk management means identifying, assessing and controlling the risks involved in trading or investing.

Markets can move unexpectedly. A risk management plan can help traders understand possible losses before placing an order.

2. What Is a Stop-Loss?

A stop-loss is an order used to help limit potential losses by triggering an order when a specified price is reached.

Example: Suppose a share is purchased at ₹100 and a stop price is set at ₹95. The difference is ₹5 per share before costs.

Important: A stop-loss does not guarantee execution at the exact stop price. In a fast market, the actual price may be different.

3. What Is the Risk-Reward Ratio?

The risk-reward ratio compares the potential loss on a trade with its potential profit.

Formula: Potential Loss : Potential Profit

Example: If the planned risk is ₹100 and the potential profit is ₹200, the risk-reward ratio is 1:2.

This ratio does not guarantee that the potential profit will be achieved.

4. What Is Position Sizing?

Position sizing means deciding how much of an asset to trade, considering the available funds and the amount of risk.

A hypothetical example: if the planned risk budget is ₹200 and the estimated risk per share is ₹10, the simple position-size calculation is 200 ÷ 10 = 20 shares.

This simplified example excludes transaction costs, slippage and gaps in market prices.

5. Common Risk Management Mistakes

  • Trading without understanding the risks.
  • Using borrowed money without considering the consequences.
  • Ignoring transaction costs and taxes.
  • Assuming that every trade will be profitable.
  • Increasing trade size after a loss to try to recover money quickly.
  • Relying on unverified tips or guaranteed-profit claims.

6. Before You Consider a Trade

  • Understand the instrument being traded.
  • Know how much money could be lost.
  • Consider liquidity and market volatility.
  • Understand the order type and its limitations.
  • Have a clear plan rather than acting on emotion.
